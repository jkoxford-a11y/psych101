## Title decision and why

Working title, agreed after discussion with Jon and GPT:

> **Psychology: The Biological Mind Under Constraint**
> *Prediction, Construction, and Inference*

The reasoning that got here: constraint isn't a fourth item coordinate with prediction, construction, and inference — it's the *reason* the mind has to predict, construct, and infer rather than just register reality directly. An early draft title (`Psychology: Prediction, Construction, Inference, and Constraint`) listed all four as a flat comma series, which misstates the book's actual thesis by making the governing condition look like a peer of the mechanisms it causes.

The two-tier cover structure fixes that: the main title states the thesis (biological mind, operating under constraint), the subtitle names the three mechanisms that follow from it. "Biological" was added because it signals genre fit for an intro psych survey text — confirm with Jon whether that word holds across the whole book or oversells chapters that are more cognitive/social/developmental than strictly biological; if it's a stretch, drop it rather than reopen the whole title debate.

## Copyright-page citation (APA)

```
Oxford, J. (2026). Psychology: The biological mind under constraint.
```

Only the primary title clause is capitalized-first-word per APA reference style (everything after the colon is lowercase except proper nouns). The mechanism subtitle ("Prediction, Construction, and Inference") is cover/interior-title material, not part of the formal reference-list entry — most style guides don't stack two colon-delimited subtitles in a citation. If Jon wants the full precision in the formal citation anyway, an em dash works: `Psychology: The biological mind under constraint—prediction, construction, and inference.`

## AI Collaboration Statement (draft, for copyright/front-matter page)

First person is correct here — this is Jon speaking as author in front matter, not chapter prose, so it's exempt from the no-first-person chapter-voice convention.

```
AI Collaboration Statement

This textbook was developed through extensive collaboration with generative
and agentic AI systems. AI tools contributed substantially to drafting,
revision, research support, source checking, coding, visual development,
instructional design, and quality assurance. I directed the work, made the
substantive and editorial decisions, and take responsibility for the final
content.
```

Do not make an AI system a coauthor. The distinction that matters is responsibility, not labor share: Jon selected the argument, decided what belonged in the book, evaluated claims, taught the course 21 times, and takes responsibility for the finished text. Naming specific systems (ChatGPT/Codex, Claude/Claude Code, etc.) belongs on a separate, later production-notes page rather than in this statement or on the cover — tool identities will keep changing across editions and the copyright page shouldn't need reissuing every time a model changes.
