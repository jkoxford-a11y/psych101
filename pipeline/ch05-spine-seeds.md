**The gap.** `self` appears twelve times in the body and every one is a modifier on something else. The chapter-map row assigns "self-modeling ... as constructed processes." `evidence-commitments.md` L64 defers the strong self claim *to this chapter specifically*. Nothing here builds it.

> Classic psychedelics act strongly at cortical serotonin 5-HT2A receptors. Wakefulness often remains, while perceptual content, emotional meaning, time experience, and **the ordinary sense of self** may change.

That phrase is doing load-bearing work on a construct introduced nowhere.

**Proposal — new subsection closing Section 3, after "Content is more than external input" (~120 words), attributed, hedged:**

> The same logic runs one step further, and this is where it gets uncomfortable. If what you see is the brain's best interpretation of noisy input, the same should hold for the brain's interpretation of the organism doing the seeing. Anil Seth has argued exactly this: the experienced self is another model — a running prediction about what kind of body this is, what state it is in, and what it is about to do. The evidence he points to is that the sense of self can be perturbed. A rubber hand can be made to feel like your own when seen and touched in sync with the hidden real one. This is an argument, not a settled result, and other researchers read the same evidence differently.

**Coupling.** If D1 is accepted, Section 4's psychedelic subsection should be re-read against it — "the ordinary sense of self may change" becomes a payoff rather than an orphan. That is a re-read, not a rewrite, and is not a separate decision.

One reference added (Seth, 2021, *Being You*, or the 2013 *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* paper — the journal source is preferable for a textbook reference list; **I have not verified which is the better citation and will check before drafting if this is accepted**).

### D4 — Neural delay and retrospective construction (Eagleman)

**Spine assignment, L392:**

> The brain takes approximately 80ms to integrate signals from different modalities and produce a unified experience. Consciousness is not a live feed; it is a slightly delayed, edited broadcast.

Count: 0 across `Eagleman`, `80ms`, `delay`.

**What it would buy.** Every construction case in the chapter is about *what* is constructed. This is the only one about *when* — the experience is assembled after the fact and stamped with a time it did not have. It is a genuinely different axis and it is cheap (~60 words in Section 3).

**The reservation, and it is real.** Eagleman is a popularizer; the 80 ms figure is a rough integration-window estimate, not a constant, and the "editing" framing is his gloss. Protocol Step 8 says a check that fails is a finding, not a gap. **I have not run this check yet.** If accepted, I verify the window against a primary source first and report back — including reporting back that it does not survive, if it does not.

**What is lost if declined.** The temporal axis of construction, which nothing else in the chapter carries. Cheap to add later; no downstream chapter depends on it.

**Answer: DECLINED 2026-07-27** — *"seems a bit much for psych101."* Recorded per Step 7 so the next pass does not re-propose it.

*What it was:* Eagleman's postdictive account — the brain buffers signals with different conduction latencies and back-dates the assembled moment, so conscious experience is not a live feed.

*Why it was seeded:* it is the only **temporal** construction case available; every other case in the chapter constructs *what* is experienced, not *when*.

*Why it was cut:* scope, primarily. But the verification pass also found two independent problems worth preserving. **First,** the 80 ms figure is a representative multisensory integration window, not a constant; it varies by task and modality pairing. **Second and more serious:** Eagleman's own "delayed, edited broadcast" gloss contradicts a stance the chapter takes forty lines earlier at L97 — *"There is no internal observer who watches the brain's screen."* A broadcast implies a receiver and an editor. That is the Cartesian theater the chapter just refused. The postdictive claim also rests on the flash-lag effect, which has at least four competing explanations (motion extrapolation, differential latency, temporal averaging, postdiction) and has not been settled in postdiction's favor.

*What is lost:* nothing downstream. If this is ever reopened, take the **conduction-latency demonstration only** (nose and toe touched simultaneously feel simultaneous despite ~20–30 ms of extra nerve to cross) and drop both the 80 ms figure and the broadcast metaphor. That version is mechanical, safe, and does not contradict L97.
