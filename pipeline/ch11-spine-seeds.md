### The spine question — replaced

The spine's own wording and my two alternatives were all about *reading* other people. They covered Sections 1, 3, and 5 and left the entire back half of the chapter unframed. The instructor's frame fixes that:

> **Why do we care what other people think — and what makes that stop?**

The second clause is what earns it. The chapter's back end is caring switching off or inverting — bystander non-intervention, deindividuation, obedience despite visible distress, prejudice, discrimination, aggression. Nothing in the previous seeds framed any of it.

Section mapping — the test the previous wording failed:

| Section | What it answers |
|---|---|
| 1 — Social cognition | we read people fast *because* they're consequential; speed is why we're wrong |
| 2 — Influence | caring, operating: consensus and authority move us |
| 3 — Groups | we care about *some* people — the boundary is the mechanism |
| 4 — Helping and harming | the conditions that switch it off, and the evolutionary limits on who gets it |
| 5 — Personality | how much, and when, varies by person |

**Consequence for #8:** kin selection and reciprocal altruism stop being a levels-of-explanation catalog and become the *evolutionary answer to "why only sometimes."* Caring is bounded by relatedness and by repeated interaction. This is why both stay.

**The Milgram opener was always asking this question.** "I would never do that" is a claim about caring; the study shows people who did care continuing anyway. The opener needs no restructuring — only the spine question landing at the end of it.

**Discipline required:** every answer must be a *named condition* — anonymity, diffusion of responsibility, group boundary, authority structure, situation strength. Phrased loosely, "why do we care" drifts toward a chapter about empathy. Mechanistic answers keep it rigorous.

### The engine sentence — final

> **Other people are the most important, most ambiguous, and least neutral thing your brain ever has to model — and unlike a rock, they are modeling you back.**

*"Model" replaces "process"* (instructor's note) — it is the project's own vocabulary and sets up "modeling you back" rather than colliding with it. "Input" dropped as redundant once "model" carries the verb.

### Section 2 — Social Influence

- **Spine claim:** **conformity is calibration, not weakness.** Other people's answers are legitimate evidence. That reframe is the single biggest voice/story upgrade available in this chapter, and the current text is one sentence away from it.
- **Lead with:** the smoke-in-the-room example (already present) — but the *interesting* version is that going along is often correct. Asch is unusual precisely because the group is visibly wrong.
- **Arc:** setup (using other people as data is smart) → tension (Asch builds a case where the data is wrong and people take it anyway) → payoff (**and some of them still saw the line correctly — which means what you believe and what you do came apart**).
- **Payoff line seed:** *Some of Asch's participants were genuinely persuaded. Others saw the line perfectly well and said the wrong number anyway. That gap — between the model and the mouth — only opens when there's another person in the room.*
- **Asch taxonomy — CLOSED, not pursued (2026-07-25).** Three searches produced only study-guide secondaries repeating a three-way perception / judgment / action scheme, none citing the monograph; the one full text retrieved reports 13 of 50 participants as "20%" (it is 26%). Decision: **drop it.** The taxonomy is a catalog, which is what the spine says to prune, and nothing a student needs depends on Asch's category names. Use the safe version — conformity researchers distinguish cases where consensus genuinely updates belief from cases where only public behavior changes. No taxonomy attributed to Asch, no percentages beyond his headline results.

- **NEW — the Asch replication boundary. Verified, and this chapter needs it.**

  The chapter currently reports "about 75% conformed at least once... closer to one-third across all critical trials" with no boundary of any kind. Meanwhile the repair pass corrected Milgram's mechanism, corrected the Stanford Prison Experiment, bounded stereotype threat, and replaced the deindividuation account. **Asch is the only classic left standing unqualified**, and it is load-bearing for Section 2. That is an inconsistency in the chapter's own evidentiary standard.

  The chapter-ready instrument is a meta-analysis, not a failed replication:

  > **Bond, R., & Smith, P. B. (1996). Culture and conformity: A meta-analysis of studies using Asch's (1952b, 1956) line judgment task.** *Psychological Bulletin, 119*(1), 111–137. doi:10.1037/0033-2909.119.1.111 — 133 studies, 17 countries, 4,627 participants. Two findings: conformity in U.S. studies has **declined since the 1950s**, and conformity is **higher in more collectivist countries**.

  Secondary, optional, and weaker — a single dramatic replication failure rather than pooled evidence: Perrin & Spencer (1981), *British Journal of Social Psychology, 20*, 205–209, the "child of its time" study (1 conforming response in 396 trials with UK science students). Useful as an anecdote, not as the evidentiary basis. Prefer Bond & Smith.

  **Why this strengthens the seed rather than undercutting it.** If conformity magnitude moves with cohort and culture, that is evidence *for* the calibration reading: people are weighting social consensus as evidence, and the weight depends on who they are and when they live. A fixed human weakness would not vary like that. Asch's finding survives — its size is a variable, not a constant.

  **Seam opportunity:** the chapter already carries a cultural boundary on the FAE (Choi, Nisbett, & Norenzayan, 1999) in Section 1. Bond & Smith is the same move in Section 2, and pairing them lets one sentence carry both — *even the "basic" social tendencies develop inside a culture.*

  **DECIDE:** add Bond & Smith boundary / add it with Perrin & Spencer as color / leave Asch unqualified: ____________________

## Cross-cutting thread with no home yet

## How to integrate this with the existing text

Measured against the post-repair source. Sections 1–5 are **4,233 body words**; the prune candidates below total **812**; the genuinely additive spine content is roughly **250–300 words** now that two threads are cut. The word budget is not the binding constraint people assume it is.

The governing point: **most of this is reordering, not adding.**

### Mechanism 1 — Invert lead-and-definition. Free, highest yield.

Every section already contains the right lead image; it just arrives *after* the definition. Section 1 defines dispositional/situational attribution, then defines FAE, then gives you the driver cutting you off. Section 2 defines conformity, then informational influence, then the smoke in the room. Section 4 is the exception and reads better because of it.

The story material is present and well chosen. It is sequenced as *illustration of a term* rather than as the thing the term explains. Flipping that costs zero net words and does most of the work `AGENTS.md` describes under "lead with the image or the stakes."

*Verified safe:* `pipeline/lint_chapters.py` does not enforce term position or bolding placement, so the inversion won't trip the linter. Key-term extraction is by section, not by sentence order.

### Mechanism 2 — Put the spine in the seams, not in blocks. ~10 sentences.

The connective tissue belongs at transitions, where the chapter currently stops and starts a new definition. Priority seams:

| Seam | What the sentence carries |
|---|---|
| FAE → self-serving bias | same compression, aimed outward vs. inward |
| Section 1 → Section 5 (the plant) | this is a sampling problem; you'll see it again |
| Asch → Milgram | consensus as evidence vs. authority narrowing the action set |
| polarization → groupthink → stereotypes | groups don't average, they concentrate |
| Section 3 mechanisms → belonging | why any of this has force |
| Section 5 → close | the payoff on the Section 1 plant |

"Attribution is compression" and "conformity is calibration" land here — doing visible work rather than announcing themselves.

### Mechanism 3 — Retrofit the apparatus you already have. Free.

The chapter already has 4 Stop and Retrieve, 2 Think About It, 3 Do Not Confuse, and 17 review questions. Nearly all test **term discrimination** — is this polarization or groupthink. Converting three or four to test **relations** — why is the FAE the same mistake as judging a trait from a single act — installs the spine in the retrieval layer at zero word cost.

This is the most underused surface in the chapter, and the audit's note about assessment not representing the chapter's breadth points at the same place.

### Mechanism 4 — New prose. The only real expense; one thread needs it.

**Belonging** is the only thread that can't ride on existing sentences, because Section 3 contains no motivational statement to attach to. Budget ~150 words. Belief/feeling and source amnesia can be absorbed into seams and the existing ELM and prejudice passages.

### Thread A (original framing — superseded)

*Instructor comment [0], on the conformity section.*

> "Humans and chimps rely heavily on coalitions. Visible dissent is bad for solidarity. Groups tend to conventions for appearance, like prototype identities in adolescents, greasers or jocks, or facial abrasion/scarring in some African tribes. Going with the group increases the strength of the group. Also makes other group dynamics happen. So yes, groups have info, but groups also have identities that apply to its members."

**Why this is a real gap.** The chapter frames conformity through two channels: informational (other people as evidence) and normative (avoiding personal rejection). The instructor is pointing at a third thing that is neither. Going along **constitutes** the group — visible dissent damages solidarity, and shared conventions of appearance mark membership. Greasers and jocks, adolescent prototype identities, and ritual scarification are all the same operation: costly, visible signals of which coalition you belong to. For a hominin, coalition strength is not a social nicety; it is survival infrastructure. The chapter currently has no vocabulary for this, and it is *upstream* of the belonging thread rather than the same thing — belonging is the individual's stake in being included; coalition maintenance is the group-level function that stake serves.

**Where it would go:** Section 2, alongside informational and normative influence. It also strengthens Section 3 — in-group favoritism and minimal-group findings make more sense if conformity is partly coalition-signaling.

### Thread B — Dissonance as model updating, not discomfort management

*Instructor comment [18], on cognitive dissonance.*

> "So your predictions say one thing and your behavior another… Does the behavior make new predictions? So you expect one thing, but by doing something else, you change what your brain is predicting, because circumstances have now changed."

**This is sharper than what is in the chapter, and it is spine-native.** The chapter currently teaches dissonance the standard way: inconsistency is uncomfortable, discomfort motivates reduction, so a belief moves. The instructor's version reverses the causal weight. Your own behavior is evidence about the world. Having said the boring task was interesting for one dollar, the system now has a new observation to account for — and with no external justification available, the available inference is that the task was somewhat interesting. Festinger's participants did not paper over an inconsistency. They updated on data they had produced themselves.

That framing makes dissonance a straightforward instance of the trunk's own loop (prediction → action → new evidence → update) rather than a bolt-on motivational mechanism, and it explains the dose-response result the standard account has to add a premise to reach: weak justification leaves more unexplained behavior, so more updating is required.

**Constraint that applies.** `theoretical-spine.md` §7 forbids collapsing prediction error, dopamine, emotion, and stress into one mechanism. This thread is defensible because it stays inside *prediction and updating* and does not reach for stress physiology or dopamine. Comments [19] and [21] — dissonance as stress from mismatch, and Freud vindicated via superego conflict — do cross that line and are recorded in `PROJECT_BACKLOG.md` rather than seeded here.
