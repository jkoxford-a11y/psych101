## Step 2 — The structural finding

Section 2, `### What Sleep May Do`, opened on this sentence (former L104, 34 words):

> No single account fully explains why sleep exists. Three of the strongest — restoration, an evolutionary
> trade-off, and memory reorganization — carry different amounts of support, but each has a concrete case behind it.

Restoration and memory reorganization are **proximate** accounts — what the system does. The evolutionary
trade-off is an **ultimate** account — what selective regime built a system that does it. They are not three
answers competing on one evidence scale; two answer one question and one answers another. Ranking them together
invites the student to read the evolutionary account as the weak one. It is not weaker — it is answering a
different question, and Lima et al. (2005) supports it well.

`theoretical-spine.md` L256 names this exact move as *"the most common framing failure in the base drafts."*

**Second, independent defect surfaced by the same look.** The glymphatic paragraph (former L116) opens
*"The restorative account gained a concrete mechanistic boost in 2013…"* — a callback to former L110a across two
intervening paragraphs. The mechanism was separated from the account it exists to support.

## Decisions

### Decision 1 — Spine question. ACCEPTED.

**What it was.** Chapter 6 was the only chapter in the book with no student-facing spine question. §8's entry was
three mechanism bullets and nothing a student could be asked.

**Installed:**

> **What is your brain doing that it cannot do while you are awake?**

Added to `theoretical-spine.md` §8 as the `**Student-facing spine question:**` line the entry lacked, and prepended
to `## Where This Fits` (L24), mirroring Chapter 8's placement of its own question as that section's first sentence.
**+13 words.**

**Why this one.** Every section answers it: circadian timing must anticipate rather than react; consolidation
needs offline replay; glymphatic clearance needs the slow-wave state; regulatory reset needs the night. The
opener at L13 already closes on its declarative form — *"the brain doing real biological work it cannot do quite
the same way while awake"* — so the two now read as question and answer.

**Alternatives considered and declined.** *"Why is being unconscious for a third of your life worth the risk?"* —
sharper hook and would have forced Decision 2, but Sections 1 and 3 answer it only indirectly, so more seam work.
*"Why can't you make up for lost sleep by trying harder?"* — most student-facing, matches Section 1's lever table
and Section 3's "not a character flaw" close, but Section 2's architecture and dreams material does not answer it.

### Decision 2 — The false ranking. ACCEPTED, without the labels.

**Installed** at the head of `### What Sleep May Do` (52 words, **+18**):

> Why does sleep exist? The question has two halves. What does sleep do for the machinery — and why would an animal
> risk a third of its life unconscious to get it? Restoration and memory reorganization answer the first. The
> evolutionary trade-off answers the second. They are not three theories competing for one prize.

**"carry different amounts of support" was dropped deliberately.** The claim is true, but the
`#### Do Not Confuse: Strong Sleep Science vs. Sleep Hype` callout (L158) already does that work explicitly and
better. Carrying it at the head of the section is what produced the false ranking.

**Why the labels were held back.** The agent's first recommendation was to install `proximate`/`ultimate` in bold
here and add "6" to Chapter 1's list, making Chapter 6 the pilot for the propagation. That recommendation was
withdrawn on the argument's own terms: if Chapter 6 gets the labels and Chapters 7, 8, 11, and 13 then do not,
Chapter 1's sentence becomes wrong in *both* directions — naming five chapters that mostly do not deliver, and
omitting two that do. Given that the propagation has sat unstarted on the backlog since Chapter 1's pass, that is
the likely end state, and it is worse than today's.

The false ranking was the real defect. The labels are optional to fixing it. **The rewrite is upgradeable** —
dropping the two bold terms into the second and third sentences is a one-sentence edit whenever the propagation
actually runs. Chapter 6 is pre-wired for it.
