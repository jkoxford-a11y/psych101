## Findings that are not seeds

**Finding 1 — the social baseline anchor was the wrong study, and the search found better.** `theoretical-spine.md` §8 names Coan, Schaefer & Davidson (2006) as the anchor. It is n=16, all white, all married, highly satisfied, Madison WI. Introducing it would land three paragraphs after this chapter tells students a 37-person oxytocin experiment "does not establish oxytocin as the general mechanism by which social support protects everyone" — the Ch. 11 Asch inconsistency in reverse.

Five alternatives were evaluated. Selected: **Hostinar, Sullivan & Gunnar (2014), *Psychological Bulletin* 140(1), 256–282** — a translational review of social buffering of the HPA axis across animal models and human studies, infancy to adulthood. It is review-level rather than one small study; it is HPA, which Section 3 already teaches and Ch. 3 introduced; it spans development, so it is the bridge this chapter already promises; and it names oxytocinergic systems as a putative mediator, which turns the orphaned Heinrichs paragraph from a debunk into a small study bounded by a review.

Also available if you want an effect size instead of a review: Thorsteinsson & James (1999), *Psychology & Health* 14, 869–886 — experimental manipulations of social support during laboratory stress, 22 studies, 56 effect sizes, N=1,167, reduced reactivity across heart rate, systolic and diastolic BP, and cortisol. Strongest causal warrant available; no mechanism story, so it makes the claim true without making it memorable.

Rejected: Coan et al. (2017) fMRI (n=110) — best *findings* for this audience, but adds an imaging paradigm to a chapter already carrying a lesion case and two fMRI-based claims, in the one section the audit flagged for thin citation. Beckes & Sbarra (2022) state-of-the-science — the authors' own framing is "evidence is mounting," which is honest and tells you the payoff is thin. Hostinar, Johnson & Gunnar (2015) — parental buffering losing potency at mid-puberty is a striking finding for readers who just came through it, but N=40 and 41 and the adolescent result is a null at that size. Let the 2014 review carry the developmental shift.

**Finding 2 — the chapter already promises this material twice and cannot currently keep the promise.** "Where This Fits" says *"Chapter 10 showed how relationships can support regulation across development."* The Connections table says the same. Chapter 10 does deliver a version, in your voice:

> Caregiver and infant fall into a physiological duet—**biobehavioral synchrony**—coordinating gaze, vocal timing, arousal, and physiology (Feldman, 2007). **For a while, the caregiver's regulated body is the infant's regulation system, borrowed from the outside.** Over months and years, the child internalizes capacities that were first shared.

That sentence is social baseline theory, for infants. The gap is not a missing thread — it is a missing continuation. Installing this is a repair, not an addition.

**Finding 3 — the spine's own Ch. 12 note overclaims relative to its own §7.** The hedonic-treadmill note prescribes the dopamine anticipation mechanism. §7 forbids generalizing dopamine RPE to other emotional systems, and the audit lists that exact mechanism under "must correct." The chapter chose §7 and is right. **The spine note needs amending, not the chapter.** Not routed as a decision because it changes nothing in Ch. 12; flagged so it is not re-proposed.

**Finding 4 — Grupe & Nitschke verifies, and should still be declined.** `theoretical-spine.md` §3 flags it "verify before citing." It verifies: Grupe & Nitschke (2013), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 14, 488–501, real and well cited. But it is about *pathological* anxiety, so using it here imports the diagnostic frame the audit's Ch. 13 Connections row explicitly bars. Recommend closing the flag by declining it in Ch. 12 and noting it as a Ch. 13 candidate. A verification that clears the citation and fails the use.

**Finding 5 — Levin (2019) is real, which makes two documents stale.** Levin defines "cognitive light cone" as the outer spatial and temporal boundary of the largest goal a system can work toward. So the audit's must-preserve requirement that the term keep "the course-specific label," and `theoretical-spine.md` §3's "course-specific metaphor, not a standard research or diagnostic term," are both overtaken by a correct attribution. See DECIDE 8.

## Section seeds

### Opener — "Emotions happen to you"

- **Spine claim:** the reflex model is right about the speed and wrong about the history. Something has to decide the alarm is worth sounding, and that something has been learning for years.
- **Lead with:** the smoke alarm, unchanged. Audit protects the image, the direct address, and the constructed-does-not-mean-chosen distinction. It is already the best cold open in the chapter.
- **Arc:** setup (event → circuit → feeling) → tension (same event, two people, two feelings) → payoff (it isn't reacting to the world, it's reacting to what the world might mean).
- **Payoff line seed:** *The alarm in your kitchen reacts to smoke. You react to what smoke might mean — and nobody arrives at that with the same history.*
- **Prune candidate:** none. This section is 283 words and earns them.
- **Watch:** §C must-preserve. Do not restore "emotions are constructed predictions, not hardwired triggers" — §7 bars presenting construction as settled, and the removed version asserted it.

### Section 1 — The Regulated Body

- **Spine claim:** regulation runs ahead of need. Allostasis is prediction pointed inward — the same machinery Ch. 4 aimed at perception and Ch. 8 at memory, aimed at the body. **This is where the compression lens installs.**
- **Lead with:** the thermostat that learns your schedule — with its verbs back (Reclaim 2). It currently arrives after two paragraphs of definition.
- **Arc:** setup (you assume the body corrects errors) → tension (it moves first, before the error) → payoff (feeling is part of getting ready, not a report on what happened).
- **Payoff line seed:** *By the time you feel anything, your body has already moved. Feeling is not the report on what happened — it is part of getting ready for what might.*
- **Compression seam seed (DECIDE 4):** *Two numbers — pleasant or not, wound up or not — standing in for everything happening in a body. That is a summary, and summaries leave things out. It is also why you can be certain you feel terrible and have no idea why.*
- **Prune candidates:** P2 (L55, the two interchangeability examples, 22 words), P7 (L69's closing sentence, 17 words — it restates the sentence before it).
- **Watch:** body budget is a metaphor and nothing else. §C, §H figure 12.1, and §K all bar one measurable currency, one literal tank, and quasi-diagnostic use. The compression seam must describe core affect as a *summary*, not as a meter. Reclaim 2 touches a §C must-preserve row.

### Section 2 — How Does a Feeling Become an Emotion?

- **Spine claim:** the same bodily state becomes different emotions depending on which concept the brain brings to it — and the concept you bring changes what you do next. Spine §3's formulation: concepts predict meaning, emotions predict mattering.
- **Lead with:** the granularity examples. Anxious / frustrated / disappointed currently arrive at the *end* of the section, after the theory table and S.M. They are the concrete hook and they are in the wrong position.
- **Arc:** setup (you have a feeling and a word for it) → tension (the word changes the action) → payoff (precision isn't vocabulary, it's a different instruction).
- **Payoff line seed:** *"I feel bad" tells you nothing about what to do next. "I'm disappointed" tells you to revise an expectation. "I'm frustrated" tells you to change the approach. Same discomfort, different instructions.*
- **Action-readiness seam seed (DECIDE 5):** this is where action tendency stops being a table cell. The line above already carries it — *instructions* is the action-readiness claim in student language. One further sentence if you want it named: *Every emotion arrives with something already leaning: fear toward getting out, anger toward pushing back, shame toward getting small.*
- **Prune candidate:** P1 (L88 entire, 59 words) — the largest single prune and the one with a consequence. See DECIDE 7.
- **Watch:** §7 — do not say emotion and cognition are separate systems; do not say concepts are explicit and emotions implicit; do not present construction as settled. Constructionism must be named once as the chapter's chosen lens (§E High) — if P1 goes, that has to land somewhere else.

### Section 2, Classic Study — Patient S.M.

- **Spine claim:** the case separates knowing from minding. She can state that a situation should be frightening without the state arriving.
- **Lead with:** the snakes. Currently the walkthrough opens on a question about bilateral damage.
- **Arc:** setup (she knows the facts) → tension (nothing follows from them) → payoff (and then CO₂ produced fear anyway, so there is more than one route in).
- **Payoff line seed:** *She can tell you the snake is dangerous. She just doesn't mind. Knowing and minding turn out to be two different jobs — and the CO₂ result shows the amygdala is not the only way into the second one.*
- **Prune candidate:** none.

### Section 3 — Stress

- **Spine claim:** stress is mobilization for a predicted demand. What makes human stress different is not the machinery but the range of things that can count as a demand.
- **Lead with:** the zebra (Reclaim 3). The section currently opens on the Lazarus and Folkman definition. Sapolsky's zebra contrast is a voice-brief §4 signature example and it now survives only in Further Reading.

Light cone attribution: Levin, and see DECIDE 8.

### Section 4 — Coping

- **Spine claim:** coping is a matching problem. What you can change determines what you should try — and other people change what the trying costs.
- **Lead with:** the two-step case. Regulate enough to act, then act. It is the chapter's central skill and the lab already practises it.
- **Arc:** setup (you assume there's a right way to cope) → tension (it depends on what's changeable, and on what state you're in) → payoff.
- **Payoff line seed:** *There is no best coping strategy, the way there is no best tool. There is only what the situation will let you change — and whether you are currently in a state to change it.*
- **Social buffering seam seed (DECIDE 3):** *Chapter 10 said it already: for a while, the caregiver's regulated body is the infant's regulation system, borrowed from the outside. You never entirely stop borrowing. Reliable people do not only make a hard thing feel better — they change what the hard thing costs you to handle.* Anchored on Hostinar, Sullivan & Gunnar (2014).
- **Prune candidates:** P4 (L121 entire, 27 words — the payoff line above replaces its job), P5 (L177's closing sentence, 17 words — the Hostinar review supersedes it), P3 (L76's opening hedge and closing sentence, 22 words).
- **Watch:** do not use Hostinar to cash out the body budget. "Here is the meter" is the overclaim §C and §H bar, and it is the tempting payoff for L55 and L76 as well. Hostinar is a review — do not upgrade it to a single demonstrated mechanism, and do not let "changes what it costs" harden into a measured quantity. If the stranger contrast ever comes up, Coan's own discussion says stranger contact probably does regulate threat response in some people some of the time; a flat "strangers don't help" would be wrong and unkind to a student thinking about a counsellor.
